# Sprint 001: learning

## An integration is blocked by whoever owns nothing

### What happened

Two vendors, each with its side ready, each pointing at the other, for almost six months. Nothing on
either side was technically missing. What was missing was a definition of what crosses between them,
and someone with the authority to decide when they disagreed.

### Why this matters for product people

Melvin Conway observed in 1968 that organizations design systems that mirror their communication
structures. When two vendors only talk through the client, the client is the only place where the
integration can be defined. If nobody on the client side takes that role, the project waits.

That role is product work, even when the title says something else: understand what each user needs
from the boundary, decide what crosses, write it down, and arbitrate.

### How to apply it

When an integration stalls between two parties:

1. Ask each side what it expects to receive, field by field, and write both answers next to each other.
2. Mark every field where the answers differ or where nobody knows the source.
3. Bring the business owner of each disputed field to decide it, not the vendors.
4. Publish the result as the contract both sides build to, with you as its owner.

### Exercise

Pick an integration you know that took longer than planned. Write down who owned the definition of
what crosses between the systems. If the honest answer is "nobody", that is probably where the time
went.
